# Narrative Style Guide

> **Retired 2026-10-27.** No chapters are written. This guide is kept for two things only: the Punctuation rules, which apply to every page in the archive, and the Fidelity rules, which still govern what may be recorded. The viewpoint, scene construction, dialogue, combat, and chapter format sections no longer apply.

## Mandate

Turn the table's session notes into sword-and-sorcery prose that feels immediate, ominous, and lived-in, without changing what happened or silently inventing canon.

## Viewpoint and tense

- **Third person limited, past tense.**
- **Ensemble viewpoint.** Rotate between the PCs as scenes demand; no single PC is the protagonist.
- **One viewpoint per scene.** Do not head-hop within a scene.
- Choose the viewpoint PC with the most at stake, the clearest information, or the strongest emotional angle. Do not default to the note-taker.
- The reader should know only what the viewpoint character can perceive, remember, or reasonably infer.
- Do not use the narration to reveal another PC's private thoughts, an NPC's secret plan, or a fact the viewpoint character has not learned.
- If a scene cannot be told from a chosen PC's viewpoint without breaking that limit, use a different PC, use a clearly separated POV section, or ask.

## Howard-adjacent mode

Aim for the pressure and texture of Robert E. Howard's sword-and-sorcery: concrete sensory detail, active verbs, momentum, ancient dread, barbaric splendor, and a world that feels older and harsher than the people in it.

Do:

- Use strong nouns and verbs.
- Keep sentences varied: short for impact, longer for texture.
- Write violence with consequence, not choreography.
- Let dread build through implication, sound, smell, and wrongness.
- Use the campaign's own vocabulary for places, peoples, gods, and objects.
- Let humor come from character and situation, not anachronistic banter.

Avoid:

- Fake-archaic dialogue (`thee`, `thou`, `mayhap`) unless the table already uses it.
- Purple prose, lore dumps, and adjective piles.
- Tolkien-style high fantasy pastiche.
- Modern therapy-speak, memes, or jarring contemporary slang.
- Em dashes. Not in prose, not in dialogue, not in headings. Use a comma, a semicolon, a colon, or parentheses.
- The racial and civilizational essentialism, gendered helplessness, and colonial assumptions common in Howard's era.
- Imitating Howard's exact sentences or trademark phrases.

## Fidelity rules

- Player choices and outcomes are fixed.
- Do not add or remove loot, XP, injuries, deaths, or NPC allegiances.
- Do not resolve an ambiguity in the notes without approval.
- Do not give a PC a competence, fear, or memory that contradicts play.
- Do not turn a rumor into a fact.
- Do not retroactively change a session to make a better scene.
- If the notes are thin, write around the gap or mark it `[?]` in the continuity ledger.

## What may be invented

These are allowed by default, but substantive choices should still be logged:

- Dialogue that fits the established scene and does not create a new obligation or fact.
- Interiority that is plausible for the viewpoint PC.
- Sensory texture: weather, light, smell, sound, and small physical details.
- Minor blocking and stage business.
- Connective transitions between scenes.
- Names for unnamed background details only if they are trivial and non-canonical; log them.

## What requires approval

- A new named NPC with narrative weight.
- A new location, faction, deity, language, or historical event.
- A new power, curse, magic effect, or magic item property.
- A change to who knows what, who was present, or what a scene meant.
- A retcon of an earlier chapter or session.

## Scene construction

1. Start with a concrete image, action, or pressure.
2. Establish place and stakes quickly.
3. Let the PCs' decisions drive the turn.
4. End scenes on a consequence, discovery, or hard choice.
5. Use transitions that preserve momentum rather than summarizing everything.

## Dialogue

- Keep it lean and character-specific.
- Let subtext carry tension.
- Do not use dialogue to explain rules or recap lore.
- If a line is invented, keep it consistent with what the character has already said and done.
- Log any invented line that creates a promise, threat, bargain, or new fact.

## Punctuation

Traditional punctuation only.

- **No em dashes**, anywhere. For an aside, use commas or parentheses. For a break or a correction, use a full stop and a new sentence. For a list or a reveal, use a colon.
- No en dashes in ranges. Write "1,000 to 2,500 words".
- Serial comma: use it where it prevents ambiguity, skip it where it does not.
- Dialogue interruptions take a comma or a full stop, not a dash.

## Description

- Favor the specific over the generic.
- Use at least two senses beyond sight when entering a new place.
- Describe people by what the viewpoint character would notice, not by a neutral census.
- Do not use man or woman as the default for a non-human character. Elowyn is a male elf, and prose calls him that or calls him nothing; "a man with a following" is wrong for him. The same care applies to any elf, dwarf, or other ancestry in the party.
- Generic human turns of phrase in a human viewpoint's narration, such as "the way a man steps around a grave", are allowed. They must not be attached to a non-human character as description.
- Keep exposition embedded in action, memory, or conflict.

## Combat

- Make the stakes and terrain clear before the first blow.
- Use a few decisive images rather than a blow-by-blow account.
- Track injuries, casualties, and expended resources accurately.
- Let fear, morale, and exhaustion matter.
- Do not grant plot armor or dramatic rescues that did not happen.

## Chapter format

- Title: `Chapter N — Title`
- Header block: source session, viewpoint, in-world date, and status.
- Default draft length: 1,000–2,500 words, adjustable to the session's density.
- End with a consequence or forward pressure, not a summary of the next session.

## Continuity ledger

Use [the continuity ledger](continuity-notes.md) for every substantive invention or interpretive choice. Group trivial sensory details into a single row if needed.

Status values:

- `pending` — awaiting your approval.
- `accepted` — approved and part of narrative canon.
- `rejected` — removed or replaced.
- `retconned` — superseded by a later decision.

## Revision process

1. Draft from the processed session record.
2. Check every scene against the raw notes.
3. Add or update ledger rows for inventions and ambiguities.
4. Revise for voice, pacing, and Howard-adjacent texture.
5. Ask you to review before treating any `narrative-only` element as canon.
