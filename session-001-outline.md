# Session 1, Narrative Outline

**Working title:** *Down and Out in Arakay*
**Current source:** [Session 1 record](session-001.md), [clean source](session-001-clean-notes.md), and the DM's Discord introduction (quoted in Scene 1)
**Draft target:** ~2,800–3,200 words, nine scenes, five viewpoint characters

## Working thesis

Five survivors of a lost caravan take a dubious bodyguard job in a dying trade city. The job exposes them to a guild war they do not understand, a cursed well, and a missing child. The session ends not in a dungeon but on a walk: the party has a guide, a prophecy, a price to negotiate, and something waiting under the green water.

The story is about **survival with style**, hungry people making hard, practical choices in a city that has already been abandoned by its river, its trade, and most of its luck.

## Narrative spine

1. **Arrival:** Five survivors walk out of the desert into a city that does not lift its head.
2. **Work:** A small job looks like a way to eat.
3. **Ambush:** The job is a trap, and the party discovers they are useful pawns.
4. **The well:** A local mystery becomes concrete, still, and wrong.
5. **The missing child:** The mystery acquires a name, a mother, and a price.
6. **The threshold:** The party chooses to go down, for money, for a child, or for both.

## Opening frame (DM's introduction)

The session opens from the DM's establishing text, as Anglad's experience rather than a voiceover:

- Arakay was the jewel of the Black Desert, astride the River Asser and a stop on the caravan road to **Khosura**, the purple city.
- The Asser dried up years ago. The trade went with it. Tree-lined boulevards and the merchants' quarter are now dusty, refuse-ridden streets and empty warehouses falling into ruin.
- The city lives on the few western caravans still bound for Khosura.
- The party signed on as drovers, drivers, guards, and cooks for passage and promise.
- **Zuiagirite raiders** came down on the caravan in the dunes west of Arakay, killed the merchants, and stripped it bare. Five souls got away with what they could carry.
- Penniless, they come to the **Piazza dei Rospi**, the Plaza of Frogs, named for the well at its center.

## Scene outline

### 1. The road that did not come in, Anglad

**Purpose:** Establish the loss, the company, and Arakay as a diminished place.

- Open in the dunes west of Arakay on the leavings of the caravan: burned wagons, stripped animals, the merchants left where they fell. Short, sensory, no battle replay, Anglad came away with his life and remembers the aftermath better than the fighting.
- The five of them afoot: Elowyn, Anglad, Bozz, Dagen, Ed, carrying what they could.
- The walk in: the gate, the dry bed of the Asser, the bridge over nothing, the boulevard gone to dust and refuse, warehouses with their roofs caved.
- Prices: water and bread at double, and every soul in the street knows it.
- First sight of the Piazza dei Rospi. The well at the center. Four frog statues. People crossing the plaza give it room without appearing to.
- Anglad's read: a city that has stopped arguing with its own decline.

### 2. The Broken Wheel and the Barking Dogs, Elowyn

**Purpose:** Ground the party in cheap lodging and put them in motion toward the job.

- The Broken Wheel, north-east corner of the Old Quarter. Nook the Older: small, wiry, middle-aged, hand out before bench.
- Wafik the porter and Rewen the laundress, both working under Nook's roof.
- The common room is thick with men who do not care to be looked at, so nobody looks.
- Next night, the Barking Dogs: a tavern built in a fighting pit, sand still in the floor, pit walls standing. Barking Dog the dwarf pours behind the board and hides nothing about what he thinks of his trade.
- Wakhar: goods and no iron, wanting an escort to the merchant quarter south-west of the plaza. Five gold the head. They take it.
- Elowyn's private arithmetic: too much money for a short walk, and men who pay well for little usually have a reason. He takes it anyway. He has no coin and a mouth that keeps working.

### 3. The crossroads, Bozz

**Purpose:** First real violence; Elowyn's speed, Bozz's weight, the party's competence.

- The quarter falls away in steps; houses lean together like drunks.
- Terrain laid out before the first bolt: an alley east, a street west with an awning and a stack of rolled carpets, a steep stair behind, and an oxcart set square across the way, a thing a man only marks afterwards.
- **Dagen is not surprised.** He stops, head cocked, and reads the alley and the ground before anyone else sees a thing; his warning is what puts the party in motion. No surprise round, no ambush.
- Crossbowmen at the alley mouth and the street corner. They loose late and wild, because **the party has the initiative**.
- **Elowyn goes in low and fast**, reaches the nearest crossbowman before the weapon comes level, and opens him down the middle. The man never gets his shot off. Bozz sees the whole thing, shoulder to hip, the body coming apart like butchered meat. This is the image the scene is built around.
- The rest of the volley comes anyway. A bolt rings off Anglad's armour and spins away. Another sticks in Bozz's shield. A third misses everything and snaps past the literator, who is standing alone in the middle of the thoroughfare, astonished, before he remembers to run.
- Anglad cuts down a second crossbowman. The rest break and run rather than stand and reload.
- The literator runs. Elowyn catches him and strikes with the flat to put him down. Bozz arrives and takes him about the waist, carries him clean off his feet, and puts him on his back with a point at his throat. The man lies still, being a man who can count.
- No PC is wounded. The literator is taken alive.
- Bozz's angle throughout: the tackle is the thing he knows he is good for, and he is aware of his own weight the way a labourer is aware of his hands.

### 4. Kollaro's truth, Anglad

**Purpose:** Reveal the party has been used, and that the guild war is bigger than the fee.

- He is a literator. The crossbows were for the merchant; the escort was an inconvenience to be shot through. Nobody names him yet; he is the man who led them, in the way a man is his job.
- Ed goes through the prisoner's pouches: **36 sp, 5 ep, 8 cp**.
- The prisoner talks, being a man who means to go on living. The merchant's name is not Wakhar. It is **Kollaro**, of the Barbers' guild. That is why the literators came out with crossbows.
- Kollaro, talking, lets slip the thing that makes the guild war a place and not just a rumour: **the literators keep their guild in the library**. Said in passing, by a man who assumes they already know. The party does not follow it up.
- **The reason for the feud stays unexplained.** The party does not know it, does not ask, and does not want to. Anglad can register the shape of a guild war without being told its cause, and the prose must not supply one. The **seat** of the literators is now known; the **cause** is not, and the two must not be confused on the page.
- Kollaro wants the literator dead. He asks plainly, standing over a bound man, as one asks a thing of people he believes to be his own kind.
- They refuse. Bound, beaten, no danger to anyone, killing is not murder. They will carry him where he is wanted; they will not open his throat in an alley.
- Kollaro does not press it. He is **understanding** of their position, and that is worse than argument: a man who takes no for an answer has already counted what the no will cost him.
- They carry the literator to Barber's Alley. The barbers take him, and the barbers are Kollaro's guild. **Kollaro names him there: Mahir.** Said without ceremony, a man greeting his own problem by name. That is where the party learns it, and nowhere earlier.
- Kollaro knowing the man's name is not a hint at the feud. He knows who shoots at him. The prose must not widen it into history.
- The fee: five gold promised, **ten more each** for help given and for discretion. **Fifteen apiece, paid on the spot and without a word.**
- Anglad's read, closing the scene: they spent the morning doing their employer's work, keeping him alive at the crossroads and walking his enemy into his own guild's hands. Every bargain after this one gets struck with a hand on the purse.
- Kollaro, helpful in the way of a man who wants to be thought useful, mentions a Chül in the city who buys gear they no longer need. They file it and say nothing.

### 5. Back to the Barking Dogs, Bozz

**Purpose:** The city's talk, and the difference between a story and a fact.

- After the handover they go back to the Barking Dogs in the same day. The pit is quiet, the sand scuffed, the smell of old copper in it. Barking Dog serves them without comment.
- They buy a jug and let the talk come to them. Three things about the plaza, each secondhand:
  1. **The urchins sometimes toss one of their own down the well.**
  2. **On mist-shrouded nights a thing in the shape of a man walks the neighbourhood.**
  3. **A frog monster lurks in the water and drags under whoever leans too near.** The last soul to draw water there came from the House of Cheap Delights.
- One more thing, offered as directions rather than a story: the urchins gather in a ruin north-west of the plaza.
- Nobody vouches for any of it. The drinkers tell the frog story the way men tell a story they enjoy being frightened by, and Bozz notes that not one of them has been near the well.
- Bozz's angle: he has heard a thousand tavern tales and can tell which ones have a body under them. These three have a body under them.
- They decide to go and look at the well themselves.
- **These three stories are heard here and nowhere else.** The urchins later confirm the monster and give it a name; they do not retell the three.

### 6. The well, Ed

**Purpose:** The central image; the first unambiguous strangeness.

- Late afternoon in the Piazza dei Rospi. The air is still. Locals give the well a wide berth and do not look at it; they go about their day as though the middle of the plaza were not there, and route around it without admitting the detour.
- No urchins here. The children come later, in Scene 9, and in their own place.
- Across the square, an old woman is already taking Dagen's hand (Scene 7). The two scenes happen at the same time.
- The well is old stone, ringed by four statues of horned bull frogs the size of dogs, worn soft by weather. Older than the city, men say. Cursed, men also say, and a city proves that with its feet.
- Ed lowers a lantern on a rope. Carvings line the shaft, frog shapes row upon row. No bottom that the light can find.
- Then one carving lifts its head, **quickly, a snap of movement, not a grind**, and a frog's face turns up at him and croaks.
- The others are there and see the light jump; the croak carries in the still air. Ed says what he saw.
- He hauls the lantern up and leaves, and nothing follows, and he chooses to count that a good sign.

### 7. The palm reading and the night after, Dagen

**Purpose:** Prophecy, dread, and a human anchor before the next day.

- This scene is simultaneous with Scene 6: same square, same late afternoon, while Ed is flat on his belly at the well. Do not stage it as happening afterwards.
- An old woman behind them, not yet a crone, a distinction that matters in such a quarter. She speaks in a whisper like dry leaves moving.
- She offers a reading. Dagen gives her his hand: a hunter respects a sign, and she asks nothing for it.
- She tells him they will go down into caverns and find death there, and great reward besides, and that he should keep clear of the green water.
- She goes her way with two cats at her heels. No one marks whether she looked back.
- Mid-reading, the croak comes up out of the well and across the square. The prophecy and the sound arrive together, and Dagen will remember them that way.
- The Broken Wheel, paid out of Kollaro's gold. **No coin totals are stated anywhere in the outline**, and no man's purse is counted.
- Wafik has a question for the company: are they joining the Khosuran armies? There is a war with Easterners, he says, and the Khosurans are winning it. Not a man among them has an answer for him.
- Rewen tells a stranger how she and Wafik came under Nook's roof.
- Dagen's angle on the prophecy: he does not argue with it and does not repeat it, and it sits in him like a stone in a boot.

### 8. The pillar and Batros, Ed

**Purpose:** The city's strange religious life, and the party's first purchase.

- Folk at worship about a broken white pillar, andesite or near it, carved with a kneeling calf wearing a crown of leaves, offerings heaped at the foot. They have the look of people who have found one thing in a dead city to hold to, and they do not invite questions. **No doctrine is explained.**
- Past the pillar the street turns a corner and becomes a market again, or the ghost of one. Sesame cakes, hot enough to sting. Elowyn buys with his own gold.
- The cakes buy the way to the Chül, which is worth more than the cakes.
- **Batros** is not in a shop. He is set up on the street with a blanket of goods spread on the ground, uncommon wares, none of them ordinary.
- His voice is slow and deep, one word set down after another like stones in a wall.
- His prices make a man count his purse twice. The party counts twice and keeps its hands in its pockets. He offers little for the crossbows and swords.
- Ed does not keep his hands in his pockets. He picks a **Fire Rock** out of a pile of them and pays five pieces, and is told that thrown down hard it will make a noise to wake the whole quarter.
- Among the wares, three more things. The **green rod** of some **glowing metal** is the first thing anyone sees: it gives off its own green light, and the daylight does not put it out. A piece of metal that holds light is a thing men step around. Chips of it, the Chül says, will sharpen a spell. A pouch of **silvery fabric** lies out with the rest, and it looks dear enough that nobody asks what is in it. Then a **necklace that has the look of lapis lazuli**, carrying a **cat charm**, priced at **150 gp**. The rod pulls the eye; the necklace pulls the purse. Nobody touches any of them. The prices are the whole of the encounter.
- Ed puts the stone away as a man puts away a thing he means to use. He has not yet thrown it.

### 9. The Splinter, the bargain, the argument, Elowyn

**Purpose:** Turn the well from a local mystery into a job with a human stake, and end on the walk.

- The ruin north-west of the plaza holds four urchins. Two come out for the cakes, still warm and passed round, which buys more than silver in such a place. The oldest, **Pablo**, arrives with Dio, and does the talking.
- The urchins do not retell the three tavern stories. They confirm the one that matters: the water is not safe, and the frog monster is real. **The oldest urchin says the name first: the Splinter.** It takes children, and it takes them down the well.
- The party has two swords and two crossbows off the dead men at the crossroads and no use for them. Iron for information. The children take the weapons with the plain competence of people who have handled worse.
- Ed does not like it: swords in the hands of children is a child army, whatever it is called. Elowyn answers that these children are already carrying things most adults would flinch at, and that they will be no less careful with the iron than the men it came from. Nobody else argues. Nobody agrees either.
- Between cakes and iron, the company has bought goodwill and a name. It has not bought a way down.
- Lady Danarose's son Orlando is missing, the urchins tell them. Elowyn's argument, in his own idiom: the woman has money, the well has a monster, and nobody goes down a hole for free, the advance first, then the descent.
- **Dio** is hired for a silver piece to walk them to the house. The oldest boy brings him forward. **The oldest boy gets a silver of his own** for the telling. Dio goes because it is silver and because the walk is short.
- Every urchin in the ruin is a **boy**. No girls in the pack.
- The session ends on the way: night streets, a boy in front, five strangers behind him, a mother's door somewhere ahead. **No knock, no door, no house interior.**

## Fidelity guardrails

- The literators' motive is **unknown to the party and stays unknown to the reader**. No invented guild politics, no informer, no debt.
- The three plaza rumours are heard at the Barking Dogs, after Kollaro, and nowhere else. No urchin recites them. No bystander repeats them later for convenience.
- The palm reading is simultaneous with Ed at the well, not a later event. Dagen's scene may look back, but the two happen in the same hour and the same square.
- The Splinter's nature is not explained, named twice, or shown. It is a name children use and a thing that takes people down a well.
- Lady Danarose is not seen, described, or given a house on the page.
- No loot, XP, injury, or allegiance is added or removed. Coin off the literator: 36 sp, 5 ep, 8 cp. The party is **penniless until Kollaro pays**, and that payment is their first income since the dunes. Prices may be named (five gold the head, five for the stone, 150 for the lapis necklace, a silver for Dio); **no man's purse is ever counted on the page**.
- The literators' seat in the library is a fact the party is told. Their motive is not. Do not let the one imply the other.
- Dagen's ranger sense means the crossroads is a fight the party sees coming. The prose must not stage it as a surprise.
- No PC gains a competence, fear, or memory that play did not establish.
- Elowyn's cleave is the exception to the outline's earlier vagueness: shoulder to hip, one stroke, per your note from the table.

## Decisions (confirmed 2026-10-20)

1. **Spelling:** **Piazza dei Rospi**, per the notes. The DM's "Piazi di Raspa" is treated as the same square, misspelled in the Discord post. The outline and the record use the notes' form and gloss it as the Plaza of Frogs.
2. **Ruin bearing:** north-west, per the official rumour.
3. **Kollaro's refusal beat:** he asks once, plainly, and is genuinely understanding when refused. No pressure, no threat, no visible resentment, which is its own kind of warning.
4. **Danarose:** the session stops on the walk. No door, no house, no meeting.
5. **The Fire Rock:** five **gold** pieces, not silver.
6. **Dagen:** his ranger sense means the crossroads is not an ambush. The party acts first.
7. **Register:** grim, dry, and funny only where the table was funny. Elowyn's mouth is the pressure valve.
8. **Money:** the party is penniless until Kollaro pays, and that is their first income. Fees and prices may be named; no man's purse is counted and no totals are carried forward.
9. **Punctuation:** no em dashes. Commas, semicolons, colons, and parentheses instead. The rule is recorded in the [style guide](../narrative/style-guide.md).
