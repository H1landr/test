Party stood at Lady Danarose's door with Dio, hired for 1 sp to guide them, hoping the Lady would fund an expedition to recover her son Orlando from the Well of Frogs.

## Chronology

### 1. The Danarose estate

- House is in disrepair. Closed wooden gate; smaller side door.
- Dio does not beg. The steward is angered by his presence alone, which the party reads as urchins being blamed for Orlando's disappearance.
- A servant answers the side door and goes to fetch the steward. It is the servant who leaves the door, not Elowyn. Lady Danarose does not come to the door.
- While they wait, Ed asks Dio whether urchins really throw one another into the well. Dio says bad men do it, meaning guild men, and tells him a woman from the House of Cheap Delights threw herself in recently after her child went missing. He is genuinely confused by the question.
- The servant returns with the steward: long white beard. The household speaks H'Zhari; the steward translates regardless.
- The steward questions the party's business. Elowyn offers condolences and makes his pitch; the steward accepts their help and invites them into the central courtyard.
- Courtyard shows traditional Arakayan architecture — false windows, none facing the street — inside an estate gone to seed.
- Two guards, several servants present. Water is brought; a chair is brought for the steward.
- The steward introduces himself as **Pardoht-Ram**, Lady Danarose's steward. *(Notes also record "Pardot / Paddock / Paddington" — the player settled on "The Steward". Spelling unconfirmed.)*
- Lady Danarose enters, veiled in black mourning dress, elaborate headdress with pearls and muted gold medallions. She does not speak to the party directly; the steward translates.
- The steward confirms the party would be paid handsomely.

### 2. Terms

- Orlando disappeared **48 hours ago**. He was enamoured of the urchins and wanted to join them.
- Party asks for something of Orlando's to track him by. Servants bring a **small chest of treasure**, holding a large but uncounted amount; the Lady states this is the reward.
- Party asks for something up front. The Lady grants **Elowyn 25 gp**.
- Bozz asks about the sword: a blade of **star metal**, from Lady Danarose's late husband's service in the **Khosurian military**. Orlando carried it into the well with him.
- Lady Danarose does not want the sword back. She believes its being down there was part of Orlando's problem.
- Steward gives Dagen a parchment — a drawing by Orlando: a figure holding a curved sword forward; opposite it a dark shape with **red eyes**; **four smaller dark shapes** behind it.
- Party is shown a painting of Orlando — a ginger child.

### 3. What the Lady knows

- Ed asks about guild interference in the tunnels beneath the well. The Lady knows nothing of guilds but knows the tunnels: the deeper layers were built by **Acheron** *(notes: "Akaron")*, the demon empire that previously terrorised the land.
- Ed asks about entrances other than the well. The Lady has heard rumours of someone called **the Collector** — a powerful sorcerer living in the tunnels who collects "wonders" and information.
- Orlando wanted to befriend the urchins, but Pablo did not like him.
- Orlando took the sword to impress Pablo, then went to fight the Splinter to show off and gain standing in the street.
- Anglad asks about competition: no group like the party; some people came looking for a handout.
- Flatbread is served. Everyone eats at the table; Anglad takes a piece and puts it in a pouch to eat in private, stating plainly that he is ugly. The household seems less offended than they would have been otherwise.

### 4. Back to the urchins

- Dio does not know how to find the Collector; suggests **Pablo** is the right person to ask.
- Dio says few of the urchins liked the Collector and that they did not care about the sword.
- At the urchin ruin: **five cats**.
- Dio is tipped an **electrum piece** to keep his mouth shut.
- Pablo: the **Literators** know of hideouts in the sewers; the **Barbers** are looking for them.
- Pablo predicts a gang war soon — Barbers versus Literators.
- Barbers would pay the party for a Literator hideout.
- Party pays Pablo **5 sp** to let Dio go with them.

### 5. The House of Cheap Delights

- Party heads there to follow up the missing woman, without incident.
- The woman is **Busty Rita**; her daughter is **Nian**.
- The father, **Hiram**, went looking for Nian. He did not go down the well; the **charcoalers** may know, by the stairs to the East.
- Rita threw herself into the well **a week ago**.
- Nian went out on a misty night and was taken. She knew she was meant to stay inside and did not like doing as she was told.

### 6. Shrine of Cloacina

- En route to the charcoalers: a domed, mud-brick shrine.
- Stairway down to an oval chamber, empty. Dio names it the shrine of **Cloacina**, the sewer goddess.
- Dimly lit with candles and incense. Two channels run down the sides of the chamber into a **3 ft drain** heading down.
- A statue of Cloacina stands at the centre.
- A central drain covered by an **iron grate**; it appears to go deep.
- No shrine attendants. Anglad finds no false walls. He lights a candle before leaving.

### 7. The charcoalers

- A number of people in black robes standing around the stairs — desert folk, apparently from the **southern part of Arakay** (notes: "Ziggurat people").
- A charcoaler is paid for charcoal by a sick-looking man, who then runs off.
- Intelligence rolls: those who pass know charcoal is a drug — breathed in for a "transcendent" experience (opium).
- Ed asks to buy some. Quoted **5 gp** ("half price"); haggles and buys "**Lotus coal**" for **3 gp**.
- Ed asks after Hiram: an occasional customer. A couple of nights ago Hiram was **at the top of the stairs** — "not last week". *(Conflicts with the account given at the House of Cheap Delights.)*

### 8. The shanty and the trapdoor

- Top of the stairs leads to a far more rundown district of dilapidated buildings and shanties.
- A ruined building with a courtyard choked with desert weeds and fallen rubble; collapse looks old.
- Dagen reads sign: people have passed through here.
- **Five cats** out here too.
- Searching the yard turns up a **trapdoor**; Dagen judges it used somewhat recently.

### 9. Below

- Trapdoor opens onto a stone-carved underground chamber — formerly a storeroom, **10 × 10 ft**. Dio carries a torch.
- A circular stone stairwell leads down. Descent judged at **~40 ft**.
- At the bottom, a square **20 × 20 ft** stone room; deeper stonework is of better quality than above.
- Doorway to the **west**; corridor to the **east**, no visible end at 30 ft.
- Things piled against the **south** wall: **skulls — small skulls**.
- Elowyn listens at the door: faint **rustling** for a few seconds, then stops.
- Dagen looks down the corridor: 5 ft wide, arched ceiling, some debris, and **tracks** — many creatures, going back and forth into the dark, in the eastern corridor.

### 10. The door and the moths

- Bozz and Anglad kick in the door. It takes two kicks, which eliminates any chance of surprise; Elowyn nocks an arrow before the kicks.
- Corridor beyond leads into a larger chamber at the edge of torchlight. A door along the corridor to the south, and a junction north. The south door is the one the bloody footprints come from; the junction leads to the altar room.
- Grey, fuzzy things on the ceiling stir at the commotion. The moths are angered by the light.

**Combat — three giant lantern moths**

| Round | Event |
|---|---|
| Party 1 | Ed throws a dagger for the first hit, 2 damage |
| Moths 1 | Two miss; one knocks the torch from Dio's hand and the area goes dark |
| Party 2 | Elowyn's arrow misses; the rest of the fight is swords, with Bozz using a mace. Bozz splatters a moth; Dagen hits and a moth impales itself on his sword |
| Party 3 | Elowyn cuts a moth in half |

All but Dagen have infravision, which is how the fight continues in the dark.

Outcome: party wins. Bozz relights the torch and assists Dio; Ed lights a lantern; Anglad keeps watch; Elowyn moves down the corridor; Dagen is left without light.

### 11. The dead room and the taxidermy hall

- Elowyn listens at the door on the southern wall: no sound, but **bloody footprints** lead from the door out into the hallway.
- Dagen reads the prints: **barefoot human**.
- The room beyond: **15 × 20 ft**, ransacked, **6 ft ceiling**, pool of dried blood at the centre, signs of a campsite along the walls. Smells of death.
- The party does not search it. The room gives them all an uneasy feeling.
- Following the prints leads to a large room with a **domed ceiling, 20 ft high**, cross-shaped — 15 ft wide, 30 ft deep. Hallway south, doorway north, another door on the west side.
- The room is full of **taxidermied animals, some humanoid**; most are unfamiliar even to Dagen.

### 12. The locked room and the altar

- Party backtracks to the corridor by the death chamber. The door stands at the end of a short corridor to the north, 10 ft down. It is a standard door, and it is locked; Elowyn picks it.
- Room is 15 × 15 ft and nondescript: a **stone table** to the north, a door to the west, a **stone font** against a wall.
- Ed inspects the table and realises it is an **altar**. Probing with his dagger reveals a **hollow compartment**.
- Ed opens it and reaches in; a **poison needle** traps him. He is struck but makes his saving throw, and the needle does him no harm.
- Using his dagger, he withdraws a small bundle safely: **two clay tablets** and **five rough-cut amber gems, worth 30 gp each**.

**Cache found:** 5 amber gems (150 gp total), 2 clay tablets — divine magic scrolls of **Bless** and **Speak with Animals**. Notes state they are not usable by sorcerers. Ed carries both. If sold or traded, each scroll is worth **250 XP**, divided among the party.

- Party takes a brief **10-minute rest** in the room. No wandering monsters.
