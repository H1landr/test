## Events in order

### 1. The Danarose estate, at the door

- The house is in disrepair. The gate is wooden and closed. A smaller door stands to one side.
- Dio does not beg. Pardoht-Ram, Lady Danarose's steward, is angered by his presence alone, which the party reads as urchins being blamed for Orlando's disappearance.
- A servant answers the side door and goes to fetch Pardoht-Ram. It is the servant who leaves the door, not Elowyn. Lady Danarose does not come to the door.
- While they wait, Ed asks Dio whether urchins really throw one another into the well. Dio says bad men do it, meaning guild men. He is genuinely confused by the question.
- Dio adds that a woman threw herself into the well recently. She was from the House of Cheap Delights; her child went missing and grief overcame her.
- The servant returns with Pardoht-Ram: a long white beard. Everyone in the household speaks H'Zhari; he translates regardless.
- Pardoht-Ram asks their business. Elowyn offers condolences and makes his pitch.

### 2. The courtyard and the terms

- Pardoht-Ram accepts the party's help and invites them into the courtyard at the centre of the building.
- The courtyard shows traditional Arakayan architecture: false windows, none facing the street. The estate is otherwise fallen into disrepair.
- Two guards and several servants are present. Water is brought; a chair is brought for Pardoht-Ram.
- His office is steward to Lady Danarose.
- Lady Danarose enters flanked by two servants, in a black veiled dress and an elaborate headdress with pearls and muted gold medallions. She does not speak to the party directly; Pardoht-Ram translates.
- Pardoht-Ram states the party would be paid handsomely, and that Orlando disappeared 48 hours ago, enamoured with the urchins and wanting to join them.
- The party asks for something of Orlando's to track him with. Servants bring a small chest holding a large but uncounted amount of treasure; the Lady defines it as the reward.
- The party asks for something up front. The Lady grants Elowyn 25 gp.
- Bozz asks about the sword. It is a blade of star metal, from the late husband's service in the Khosurian military. Orlando carried it into the well with him.
- Lady Danarose does not want the sword back. She believes its being down there was part of Orlando's problem.
- Pardoht-Ram gives Dagen a parchment bearing a drawing by Orlando: a figure holding a curved sword forward, opposite it a dark shape with red eyes, and four smaller dark shapes behind it.
- The party is shown a painting of Orlando: a ginger child.

### 3. What the Lady and Pardoht-Ram say

- She knows nothing of guilds, but knows of the tunnels. The deeper layers were built by Acheron, the demon empire that previously terrorised the land.
- She knows of rumours of a person called the Collector: a powerful sorcerer living in the tunnels who collects wonders and information.
- Orlando wanted to befriend the urchins, but Pablo did not like him.
- Orlando took the sword to impress Pablo, then went to fight the Splinter to show off and gain standing in the street.
- Anglad asks about competition. Pardoht-Ram says no group like the party has come, but some people came looking for a handout.
- Flatbread is served. All the party eat at the table; Anglad takes a piece and puts it in his pouch to eat in private, stating plainly that he is ugly. The household seems less offended than expected.

### 4. Back at the urchin ruin

- Pablo is the oldest of the urchins, and the same boy the archive recorded as the unnamed oldest in Session 1.
- Dio does not know how to find the Collector and suggests Pablo is the right person to ask.
- Dio says few of the urchins liked the Collector, and that they did not care about the sword.
- Five cats are present at the ruin. Dio is tipped an electrum piece to keep quiet.
- Pablo says the Literators know of hideouts in the sewers, and that the Barbers are looking for them.
- Pablo says a gang war will come soon: Barbers against Literators. The Barbers would pay for knowledge of a Literator hideout.
- The party pays Pablo 5 sp. Pablo permits Dio to go with the party.

### 5. The House of Cheap Delights

- The party travels there without incident.
- The missing woman is Busty Rita. Her daughter is Nian; Nian's father is Hiram.
- Hiram went looking for Nian. He did not go down the well. The charcoalers may know something; they are by the stairs to the east.
- Rita threw herself into the well a week ago.
- Nian went out on a misty night and was taken. She knew she was supposed to stay inside and did not like doing as she was told.

### 6. The shrine of Cloacina

- The shrine is a domed mud-brick building. A stairway leads down to an empty oval chamber.
- Dio names it the shrine of Cloacina, the goddess of sewers.
- The chamber is dimly lit by candles and incense. Two channels run down the sides into a 3 ft drain heading down.
- A statue of Cloacina stands at the centre. A central drain is covered by an iron grate and appears to go deep.
- No shrine attendants are present. Anglad inspects the walls, finds no false walls, and lights a candle before leaving.

### 7. The charcoalers

- People in black robes stand around the stairs: desert folk, apparently from the southern part of Arakay. They are southern-quarter residents, not attached to the Ziggurat complex.
- A charcoaler is paid for charcoal by a sick-looking man, who then runs off.
- Party members who pass an intelligence roll know charcoal is a drug, breathed in for a transcendent experience.
- Ed asks to buy some. The price quoted is 5 gp, described as half price. Ed haggles and buys Lotus coal for 3 gp.
- Ed asks after Hiram. A charcoaler says Hiram was an occasional customer, and was at the top of the stairs a couple of nights ago, not last week. The charcoalers are ambivalent to detail rather than misleading.

### 8. The shanty and the trapdoor

- The top of the stairs leads to a far more rundown district of dilapidated buildings and shanties.
- A ruined building stands among the shanty. Its courtyard is choked with desert weeds and fallen rubble; the collapse looks old.
- Dagen determines that people have passed through.
- Five cats are present here, not the five from the ruin.
- The party finds a trapdoor. Dagen determines it has been used somewhat recently.

### 9. Below the trapdoor

- The trapdoor opens onto a stone-carved former storeroom, 10 × 10 ft. Dio carries a torch.
- A circular stone stairwell leads down. Dagen estimates the descent at 40 ft.
- The stairwell ends in a square room, 20 × 20 ft. The stonework deeper down is better than above.
- A doorway leads west. A corridor leads east and has no visible end at 30 ft.
- Objects are piled against the south wall: skulls, and small ones.
- Ed inspects the stonework. Elowyn hears faint rustling behind the door for a few seconds, which stops. Anglad and Bozz keep watch.
- Dagen looks down the eastern corridor: 5 ft wide, arched ceiling, some debris, and tracks of many creatures going back and forth into darkness.

### 10. The door and the moths

- Bozz and Anglad kick the door in. It takes two kicks, which eliminates any chance of surprise. Elowyn nocks an arrow before the kicks.
- Beyond, the corridor leads to a larger chamber at the edge of torchlight. A door stands to the south; a junction leads north.
- The south door is the one the bloody footprints come from, and it opens into the dead room. The north junction leads to the altar room.
- Three grey fuzzy creatures are on the ceiling and stir at the commotion. They are giant lantern moths, and they are angered by the light.
- Round 1: Ed throws a dagger and lands the first hit, 2 damage. Two moths miss; one knocks the torch from Dio's hand and the area goes dark.
- All the party except Dagen have infravision.
- Round 2: Elowyn's arrow misses. The rest of the fight is fought with swords; Bozz uses a mace. Bozz splatters a moth, and Dagen hits so that a moth impales itself on his sword.
- Round 3: Elowyn cuts a moth in half. All three are killed.
- Bozz relights the torch and assists Dio. Ed lights a lantern. Anglad keeps watch. Elowyn moves down the corridor. Dagen is left without light.

### 11. The dead room

- Elowyn listens at the door on the southern wall: no sound, but bloody footprints lead from the door into the hallway. Dagen reads them as a barefoot human's.
- The room is 15 × 20 ft and ransacked, with a 6 ft ceiling, a pool of dried blood at the centre, campsite remains along the walls, and the smell of death.
- The party does not search the room. It gives them all an uneasy feeling.

### 12. The domed hall

- Following the prints leads to a room with a 20 ft dome, cross-shaped, 15 ft wide and 30 ft deep.
- A hallway leads south, a doorway north, and another door stands on the west side.
- The room holds taxidermied animals, some of them humanoid. Most are unfamiliar even to Dagen.

### 13. The locked room and the altar

- The party backtracks to the corridor by the death chamber. The door stands at the end of a short corridor to the north, 10 ft down. It is a standard door, and it is locked. Elowyn picks it.
- The room is 15 × 15 ft: a stone table to the north, a door west, a stone font against a wall.
- Elowyn and Bozz go to the door. Ed inspects the table and finds it is an altar. Anglad guards the rear.
- Ed probes the altar with his dagger and finds a hollow compartment. He opens it and reaches in; a poison needle strikes him and he makes his saving throw. The needle is harmless once the save is made.
- Ed draws out a small bundle with his dagger: two clay tablets and five rough-cut amber gems worth 30 gp each.
- The two clay tablets are the scrolls: divine magic of Bless and Speak with Animals, not usable by sorcerers. Ed carries both, with the gems.
- The party rests in the room for ten minutes. No wandering monsters appear.
- The session ends here.

- What the Splinter is, and whether it took Nian.
- Whether the Collector is the resident of the domed taxidermy hall.
- What the four smaller shapes behind the red-eyed shape in Orlando's drawing are.

- Who the small skulls belonged to.

- Who can use the two divine scrolls.
- Whether the guild war starts before the party comes back up.
- Whether the shrine of Cloacina is truly abandoned or kept by someone unseen.
